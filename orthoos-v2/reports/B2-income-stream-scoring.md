### 1. Surgical volume via Surat funnel — 66

Verdict: still the compounding local asset, but at <₹25k net per arthroscopy
it is a volume game, not a margin game; the funnel must produce many cases,
not a few. GBP is active and word-of-mouth dominant, so digital demand is
untapped headroom. Worth the C5 machine, but no longer automatically the
single largest lever C1 claimed.
First paying customer: an out-of-town or Google-found patient booking a paid
consult that converts to surgery.
Manual minutes per ₹1,000: ~20–25 (INFERENCE: ~8–10 h total surgeon time per
₹25k case).

### 2. Medical AI evaluation / rater platforms — 76.5

Verdict: VERIFIED market is real and pays well at the physician tier —
Mercor advertises $50–180/h for healthcare professionals, with MD roles
commonly $130–175/h and specialty roles higher; DataAnnotation's medical
track starts ~$40–50/h. VERIFIED risk: pay is region-tiered — India-based
specialist rates on some vendors run far lower (reports of $3–7/h for
India-employed annotation specialists), and India-eligible postings cluster
at $20–100/h; projects pause without notice and assessments are unpaid.
Bridge and credential, not the engine — exactly as C1 says.
First paying customer: first accepted platform project (apply to 8–12,
expect 1–3 acceptances).
Manual minutes per ₹1,000: ~10 at ₹6,000/h (~$70).

### 3. Paid online second opinion — 73

Verdict: at ₹1,500–2,500 per opinion the standalone ₹/h (~₹3k) barely clears
the ₹4k/h kill line — its economics are funnel economics, converting
national demand into Surat surgeries plus de-identified portfolio material.
VERIFIED: competing Indian services price online surgical second opinions
from ~₹900 to free (hospital lead-gen), so positioning must be
"structured written opinion by a named arthroscopy surgeon + video call",
not price. Telemedicine is legal in India under the 2020 MoHFW guidelines.
First paying customer: an existing patient's relative or WhatsApp inquiry
during the Day-6 soft launch.
Manual minutes per ₹1,000: ~22 standalone (INFERENCE: 45 min per ₹2,000
opinion); far better counted as funnel cost per surgical case.

### 4. Direct clinical-AI consulting — 89 (top score)

Verdict: the premium tier — rubric design, evaluation lead, clinical
advisory for MSK/imaging-AI companies. VERIFIED: radiology/imaging-AI
expert work advertises up to $150/h+ even on open platforms, and the
funded imaging-AI startup pool is large (50+ active companies; advisory
boards are standard practice). Slow cycle: contracts come from outreach and
the editor+statistician credential, not applications — hence it scores
highest on rate and fit but not on 12-month probability.
First paying customer: one MSK imaging-AI company from the Day 31–60
20-contact outreach converting to a paid pilot (rubric design ~₹50k–1L
fixed, or retainer).
Manual minutes per ₹1,000: ~5 at ₹12,000/h.

### 5. Biostatistics / methodology consulting — 79

Verdict: VERIFIED market rates: freelance platforms $30–200/h, private
firms $200–400/h, university centres $70–310/h; Kolabtree-style project
fees $400–2,400; India-based statisticians do win international work via
these platforms. The journal editorship plus postgraduate statistics
training is a rare, verifiable combination — this is the stream where the
credentials do the selling. Deliverables are asynchronous, ideal for OT-day
constraints.
First paying customer: an ortho research group or device company needing a
protocol/analysis review — sourced via the journal network or a Kolabtree
profile in week 1–2.
Manual minutes per ₹1,000: ~8–12 at ₹5,000–8,000/h.

### 6. TallRise remote-consult funnel — 68.5 (descoped)

Verdict: genuinely high-ticket national catchment, but B0 Q11 keeps it
separate for 90 days — correctly, since one funnel must prove itself first.
Revisit at the Day 61–90 gate with the second-opinion conversion data.
First paying customer: n/a this quarter.
Manual minutes per ₹1,000: similar to #1 (INFERENCE).

### 7. Resident research-methods / thesis-statistics program — 63.5

Verdict: strong credential fit but residents are price-sensitive; ₹3L/month
needs implausible cohort sizes. Better run later as productised content
feeding #5's reputation than as a 90-day revenue stream.
First paying customer: a batch of 10–20 residents at ₹3–8k each (INFERENCE).
Manual minutes per ₹1,000: ~15–20 once materials exist.

### 8. Paid pre-op education pathways — 53.5

Verdict: lowest score — it monetises surgical volume that doesn't exist yet
at scale (<30 new OPD/month). The brief already gates it behind Day 61–90 as
an either/or against scaling second opinions. Keep gated.
First paying customer: a booked TKR/ACL patient buying a ₹2–5k pathway.
Manual minutes per ₹1,000: low once built (~5–10), but the build only pays
behind real volume.

### 9. Surgical training / faculty work — 61

Verdict: credible and disclosed-compliant, but synchronous, travel-bound and
capped by the 90 h/month budget; honoraria in India rarely clear the ₹4k/h
line once travel is counted (INFERENCE). Accept opportunistically; don't
build for it.
First paying customer: a course/faculty invitation via the journal network.
Manual minutes per ₹1,000: ~25–40 including travel.

### 10. Medico-legal expert opinions — 64.5

Verdict: VERIFIED that orthopaedics is among the highest-paid expert-witness
specialties in Western markets ($500–1,000+/h US); India-specific rates are
not publicly documented and INFERENCE puts them at ₹5–15k per opinion with
slow, adversarial timelines. Reasonable opportunistic add-on after 90 days;
not a build.
First paying customer: an advocate/insurer needing an orthopaedic opinion.
Manual minutes per ₹1,000: ~15–25 (INFERENCE).
